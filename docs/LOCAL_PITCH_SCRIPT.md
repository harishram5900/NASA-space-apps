**1 · Harish:** Quick question: who here checked the weather today? *(raise your hand, wait)* **[click]** Now imagine betting your whole year’s paycheck on it.

**2 · Harish:** That’s what every farmer does, every spring. And the weather they learned from is changing. I’m Harish, and this is Giri, Atharv and Dhishant. We’re Team AgriHaven, four high school students who used NASA data to help farmers make that bet.

**3 · Giri:** Here’s the problem. Farmers plan with what has always worked: their family’s experience. But the climate that experience was built on is changing, and no farmer can see it on their own field. So we checked NASA data for Champaign County, in the heart of Illinois corn country, a couple of hours south of here. Since the ’90s: 148 more degree-days of heat, a frost-free season 12 days longer, more hot summer nights, and a drier water balance. Farmers there are planning for a climate that no longer exists.

**4 · Giri:** And when it goes wrong, it goes badly wrong. In 2012, one hot, dry summer took Illinois corn from 157 bushels an acre to 105. A third of the harvest, gone.

**5 · Harish (step forward, slowly):** So here’s our one sentence: AgriHaven lets any farmer test-drive a crop rotation on NASA data, today and in the 2050s, before they plant it. It does four things: compares rotations, reveals the tradeoffs, shows the risk, and makes the uncertainty visible.

**6 · Atharv:** Three steps. Pick any field on Earth. See how its climate has shifted, across 13 tested indicators. Then compare rotations of ten crops, ranked by risk, soil health and cost. Dhishant?

**7 · Dhishant (about 60 seconds, live site):**
1. Map, type “Champ”, pick Champaign: “Let’s open that same Champaign County field. Type, pick, and it pulls 35 years of NASA data for that exact spot.”
2. Right now: “This season: the warmest 30 days on record here, and dry. So it tells the farmer: drill your cover crop, don’t broadcast it.”
3. Satellite lab: “From space: crop greenness, NISAR radar and soil moisture, read for this exact field.”
4. Compare & risk → 2050s · high: “And the decision. Corn-soybean looks safe today, but it uses the most fertilizer and loses the most soil. In the 2050s, hot days almost triple. We don’t hide that uncertainty. We show it.”
5. Tap 🌐, pick हिन्दी: “And it speaks 130+ languages, in one tap. Giri?”

**8 · Giri:** Everything you saw runs on NASA data: 35 years of daily NASA POWER weather; NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 projections from three climate models for the 2050s; and satellites: MODIS, SMAP, NASA-ISRO’s NISAR radar, OPERA, GPM rainfall, CERES UV and SEDAC croplands. Soil comes from SoilGrids.

**9 · Atharv:** And we turn those satellite pixels into real numbers for one field: near-infrared and heat index, radar backscatter, NDVI greenness, a thermal raster, soil moisture at three depths, the crop cycle, UV index, and the nutrients the soil supplies.

**10 · Atharv:** And we tested it. Without being told, it finds the 2012 drought and the soaking-wet 2019 spring, when farmers had just two dry days to plant.

**11 · Atharv:** So who wins? Corn-soybean is safest today, but it uses the most fertilizer, loses the most soil, and falls furthest by the 2050s. Soybean, wheat and sunflower wins on water: an 82 percent Water Certainty Rate, versus 73.

**12 · Atharv:** For one 500-acre farm there, that switch saves about $6,900 in fertilizer a year, keeps 524 tonnes of topsoil on the field, and stores 228 tonnes of CO₂ in the soil.

**13 · Harish:** Next: test it against real harvest records with extension agents, and go field by field.

**14 · Harish:** So why us? All four of us have spent time in our grandparents’ villages, watching them farm. We saw how much depends on one guess about the weather, and how often that guess is now wrong. So we did the research, and we built the tool we wish they’d had.

**15 · All four, together, slowly:** From space to soil. From data to decisions.
**Harish:** That’s Team AgriHaven. Thank you!
