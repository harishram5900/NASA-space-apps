**1 · Harish:** Quick question: who here checked the weather today? *(raise your hand, wait)* **[click]** Now imagine betting your whole year’s paycheck on it.

**2 · Harish:** That’s what every farmer does, every spring. And the weather they learned from is changing. We’re Team AgriHaven. I’m Harish, and this is Giri, Atharv and Dhishant. We used NASA data to help farmers make that bet.

**3 · Giri:** Here’s the problem. Farmers plan with what has always worked: their family’s experience. But the climate that experience was built on is changing, and no farmer can see that on their own field. So we checked with NASA data. Take Champaign County, in the heart of Illinois corn country, a couple of hours south of here. Since the ’90s, it has gained 148 degree-days of growing heat, and its frost-free season is 12 days longer. Farmers there are planning for a climate that no longer exists.

**4 · Giri:** And when it goes wrong, it goes badly wrong. In 2012, one hot, dry summer took Illinois corn from 157 bushels an acre to 105. A third of the harvest, gone.

**5 · Harish (step forward, slowly):** So here’s our one sentence: AgriHaven lets any farmer test-drive a crop rotation on NASA data, today and in the 2050s, before they plant it.

**6 · Atharv:** Three steps. Pick any field on Earth. See how its climate has really shifted, across 13 indicators. Then compare rotations across ten crops, ranked by risk, soil health and cost, in plain English. Dhishant?

**7 · Dhishant (about 60 seconds, live site):**
1. Map, type “Champ”, pick Champaign: “Let’s open that same Champaign County field. Type, pick, and it pulls 35 years of NASA data for that exact spot.”
2. Right now: “This season: the warmest 30 days on record here, and dry. So it tells the farmer: drill your cover crop, don’t broadcast it.”
3. Satellite lab: “From space: crop greenness at its peak, NDVI 0.78, radar from NASA’s new NISAR satellite, and soil moisture at three depths.”
4. Compare & risk → 2050s · high: “And the decision. Corn-soybean looks safe today, but it uses the most fertilizer and loses the most soil. In the 2050s, hot days almost triple. We don’t hide that uncertainty. We show it.”
5. Tap 🌐, pick हिन्दी: “And it speaks the farmer’s language: 130+ languages, one tap. Giri?”

**8 · Giri:** Everything you just saw runs on NASA data: 35 years of daily NASA POWER weather and NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 climate projections from three models.

**9 · Giri:** And we decode satellite pixels from MODIS, SMAP and NISAR back into real numbers for one single field.

**10 · Atharv:** And we tested it. Without being told, it finds the 2012 drought and Illinois’s soaking-wet 2019 spring, when farmers had just two dry days to plant.

**11 · Atharv:** So who wins? Corn-soybean is the safest choice today, but it uses the most fertilizer, loses the most soil, and falls furthest by the 2050s. Add a rye cover crop, and most of that risk goes away.

**12 · Atharv:** For one 500-acre farm there, that saves about $6,900 in fertilizer a year, keeps 524 tonnes of topsoil on the field, and stores 228 tonnes of CO₂ in the soil.

**13 · Atharv:** Next, we want to check AgriHaven against real harvest records with extension agents, bring in 30-metre field-level satellite data, and add more crops and regions. Harish?

**14 · Harish:** So why us? All four of us have spent time in our grandparents’ villages, watching them farm. We saw how much depends on one guess about the weather, and how often that guess is now wrong. So we did the research, and we built the tool we wish they’d had. Because every farmer deserves to see what NASA sees.

**15 · All four, together, slowly:** From space to soil. From data to decisions.
**Harish:** That’s Team AgriHaven. Thank you!

## Tips
- Slide 1 has one click: the “paycheck” line appears on Harish’s click.
- Load Champaign in a browser tab before you go up, and make sure the site is in English.
- **If the Wi-Fi fails:** play the video on slide 7 and say the same lines over it. Stop it when your lines are done (it’s 87 seconds).
- **If you’re running long:** skip slide 9 (Giri goes from 8 straight to Atharv on 10), then drop the 🌐 line.
- Time one full run with a stopwatch. Aim for 4:30 or less.
